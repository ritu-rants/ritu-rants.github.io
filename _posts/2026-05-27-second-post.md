## The end of my Ph.D.?

The end of my Ph.D. was somewhat of a drawn out process, though I tried purposefully to set it up as such. I took my comprehensive exams, for my program you take this at ~ 6 months pre-defense, in August 2025. My committee recommended I do a couple more experiments for my final project. Within a month I had finished the experiments and was writing the last manuscript of my doctoral studies. 

## Why did it take me until April 2026 to defend?

I had heard through the grapevine that the job search was looking horrendous this time around.

## Back to the logistics

Either way, given the general difficulty of finding a job, the fact I had a fellowship that would provide support until August 2026, and my desire to have a little bit of an off period over the 2026 summer, meant that I decided to wait until April 2026 to defend. There actually isn't much interesting about the defense, it is done. This timing gave me the flexibility to graduate during the Spring semester if I had a job that wanted me to start at the beginning of the summer, or to graduate during the summer and wrap up a few things from the Ph.D. if I didn't have a job or found one that wasn't starting until the fall.

One thing that was important for me, as well, was that I had the time to co-instruct the undergrad Heat and Mass Transfer course here at CU during the Fall 2025 semester, an experience that turned out to be quite fulfilling, given my interest in teaching, but also was an excellent experience to learn from and discuss during my interviews.

## Okay the job search.

So I was interested in both assistant teaching professor positions and industry positions leaving my Ph.D. Ultimately, I really enjoy teaching but I think I realize that to be a good teacher to engineers I probably need to be an engineer in an industry first. Either way, starting in September 2025 I began applying to ~3-5 jobs a week. Most industry position I applied to were in the semiconductors industry but there were also several in areas like pharma, consulting, and medical devices. On the teaching side, I put together my first application package for a teaching professor position at Tufts in Boston. I will say, the process of putting together an assistant teaching professor application package is tough and was made much easier with the support of other teaching faculty.

My first job interview was actually for this Tufts position at the end of October 2025. I really enjoyed interviewing at Tufts and have nothing but positive things to say about the department there. Unfortunately, there were a few personal reasons which meant that even if I was offered the position, I am unsure if I would have accepted it. Firstly, I was in a long-distance relationship at the time where moving to Boston instead of California was fairly questionable. Second, personally, I did not really enjoy Boston as much as I liked the department. I think I could have grown to enjoy it there but at least at the time I did not really enjoy it much. Unfortunately (fortunately?) I was not offered the position at Tufts.

So back to the abyss of applying for jobs. Of jobs that I just applied for via an online application portal, I received a interview request for only two. One was for Tufts and one was for a startup where I had a relatively poor interview experience. Aside from those I interviewed for three other positions, all three likely because I had a connection at the company. One of them was for a bio company that to this day I actually still have not heard back from. One was for an associate position at Company X, which I am sure you can figure out based on my soon to be updated LinkedIn status (spoiler alert). The last was for an assistant teaching professor position at the University of Washington.

It is quite funny how these things come in waves. I had my in-person interview, which was pre-empted by a brief zoom interview a couple weeks prior, for the teaching position on a Tuesday at the end of February and then the following monday had my in-person interview at Company X. In addition to the in-person at Company X I also had two technical 30ish minute zoom interviews prior to being invited for the in-person. Both interviews went okay. If you had asked me the day after my UW interview I would have said I bombed it. I felt pretty anxious about how I performed. With regard to the interview at Company X, I had a much better feeling about, though I tend to pick apart my performance after the fact and had some hold ups about how I responded to some technical questions.

## My decision.

I ended up getting offered both positions, though I did not know I would get offered the UW position when I was offered the position at Company X. UW was interviewing in total I think four people, and the last person was not even being interviewed until after the deadline (which they had already graciously extended) to accept the offer from Company X. I mean, all things considered, it would be foolish to decline an offer for the potential of another offer, even though I did get offered the UW position later on down the line. When I made the decision to accept the offer from Company X I tried to develop my internal justification assuming that I would get offered the UW position. Aside from the compensation reasons, I think what led me to lean towards Company X was that I do truly think that I will be a better engineering teacher in the future having gained the experience in industry that I will. One particular benefit is that this position is in technical consulting, meaning that I will get perhaps an even broader exposure to how baseline engineering principles make their way into prevalent industries. Of interest also is that by having broader connections via future clients, I may be of more use to my future students looking for internships and jobs.

I suppose this foreshadows that by all means I do plan to go back to teaching after having developed a career in consulting. One exciting potential is that Company X does support their consultants teaching once they are senior in their careers, meaning that I will be afforded quite substantial career flexibility, if I do make it to become that senior. I suppose there is always the potential that I am not cut out for consulting and fail miserably, but alas isn't that always a potential risk in life.

Either way, that wraps up my experience with the job search. Fortunately Company X is quite flexible and I am able to start my position at the end of August, allowing me to finish up my Ph.D. and fellowship stipend in a chill way through the summer. I feel pretty grateful to overall have had a really positive experience in my job search. I am no stranger to the fact that this could have been so much more frustrating, as I can see in many of my friends going through the process. Like all things, though, thank god it is fucking done.
